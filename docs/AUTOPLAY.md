# Watching a story unfold

Click **Autoplay** in the bottom dock or press **P** to let the computer guide your controlled tribe. It continues from the current turn and each household's remaining actions. The button becomes **Take control**; pressing it, **P**, or an ordinary gameplay action returns control immediately. Escape closes an open map card or menu first, then returns control when none is open.

**Next band / N** on the selected-band card also returns manual control. It cycles through living controlled households with actions remaining, centers the chosen band and leaves all budgets and the turn unchanged. It skips exhausted and newly founded households. If no household is ready, autoplay still stops and the interface asks you to end the turn when ready. The shortcut is inactive while a blocking reading or encounter sheet is open.

Open **Watch settings** on the Map to choose **1×**, **3×**, or **10×**. These request one decision every 900, 300, or 90 milliseconds; rendering and simulation work can slow the fastest setting. Each decision submits one ordinary band order or a global end-turn command. Observation speed changes neither action budgets nor the meaning of a turn. Progress is displayed in turn iterations. [Time and provisioning](HISTORY_TIME.md).

**Follow your band: on/off** controls camera follow. With follow on, the camera centers the household autoplay is currently directing when that actor changes or moves, rather than refocusing after every stationary action. With follow off, the camera remains where you leave it. The dock and selected-band card identify the current actor, and the card shows its supplies and remaining actions. The top ribbon summarizes the tribe. You can inspect the world, page through a crowded hex's group list, change map layers or useful-place glows, and open ledgers while watching. Dragging or zooming holds decisions until the gesture settles. Reading older history entries keeps your place as new entries arrive.

## Events without losing the story

**Major events: keep flowing** is the default. Dismissible adviser side cards explain discoveries, domestic lineages and changing tribal relationships while decisions continue. **Tell me more** pauses play and opens fuller counsel. Other important historical outcomes, including casualties, can still open a reading sheet in manual play or with **Major events: pause to read** enabled. Every historical account remains in the archive.

Use **Read** on a map notice, or **Events** in the ribbon and **Read** in the archive, to open the full account. Reading pauses autoplay. A paused autoplay event offers **Continue autoplay** or **Take control**; **Inspect** opens its relevant map card, ledger or landscape guide. Escape dismisses the reading sheet into manual control. **History → Events & counsel** preserves the account alongside the original history text.

Inspected units retain their identity as they travel through known land. With camera follow on, subsequent autoplay decisions return the displayed band selection to the acting household; switch follow off for sustained observation elsewhere. **Attack**, **Befriend** and **Move / meet** return control and open target-specific choices. The encounter sheet retains its actor and blocks other gameplay input; cancel it with **Leave alone / Esc**. Speed does not change encounter chances. At the fastest setting, several decisions or turns can pass during a short visual movement transition.

Opening Save, Load or New Story stops autoplay before the dialog opens. Cancelling leaves manual control in place. New and loaded stories never silently resume autoplay from a save. Autoplay continues while another application is active, so use **P** when you want it to stop; Escape first dismisses any open sheet or menu.

## How the computer plays

`AutoplayPolicy.Choose` is deterministic and read-only. In tribal play it chooses a controlled household with actions remaining, uses that household's actual position and reserves, and records its band ID with the order. Each household has two actions per turn; newly founded bands begin on the next turn. A well-provisioned household can wait while others finish their work. Once none has actions left, the policy closes the whole tribe's turn. The player's **End turn** control can also close it early.

The policy balances food, salt, animal care, shelter, local danger and productive remembered land. It gathers reserves, establishes camps, leaves depleted ground, hunts manageable prey and builds affordable animal relationships. When conditions permit, splitting transfers people, provisions and salt into a new controlled band. These daughter households receive their own orders; they are not immediately independent AI peoples.

Salt collection uses the same source and action rules as manual play. The policy seeks a safe remembered source when reserves run low, while protecting imminent food needs and avoiding stronger local threats. Collecting salt is an action at the source; passing through a crystal marker does not collect it. Food and frontier glows are visual guidance and do not grant resources or change policy state. Read their meaning in the **Food / Salt / Paths** guide. [Map controls](MAP_INTERFACE.md), [salt rules](SALT.md).

Each daughter's disposition shapes its journeys. Loyal bands seek the leader; Restless ones explore before seeking reunion; Adventurous and Separatist bands favor room away from home when supplied. Their separation deadlines differ, and being at least two hexes away shortens the more independent bands' deadlines. Autoplay can therefore produce related independent peoples instead of always bringing every household home. Reunion still requires occupying the leader's current hex. Food, salt and danger remain higher priorities. Legacy rules retain the former five-turn return policy and 8/16 drift/secession thresholds until upgraded. A surviving member succeeds a fallen leader, and autoplay continues while any controlled band lives. [Dispositions and reunion deadlines](TRIBES.md).

For manual planning, the selected-band card's **Reunion route** button displays a separate preview through remembered safe land. It shows the action cost and turn windows for that route, including the current turn's remaining budget. The route minimizes action cost rather than guaranteeing the fewest turns; food and hazards can change before arrival. This display never queues movement, switches actors or tells autoplay to follow the preview. It refreshes as the actor or leader moves and is cleared on loading a different story. Use **N** or **Take control** before guiding the household along it yourself.

Ordinary adjacent movement costs one action. Entering mountains or crossing a river costs two, including a step with both obstacles. An adjacent encounter approach uses that same cost; an encounter on the current hex costs one. The policy checks the band's remaining actions, allows for travel provisions, and routes salt and reunion journeys using those terrain costs. Mountains remain passable. Earlier commands recorded with legacy travel retain their original one-action costs. [Landscape rules](LANDSCAPE.md).

With moving-unit encounters, decisions use visible groups' actual strength, hostility, remembered contacts and approach cost. The policy avoids stronger enemies when a safe route exists, may attack a weaker hostile band, leaves neutral peoples alone, favors continuing trust with manageable animals and avoids risky dragon approaches. Independent bands and wild groups act at turn close, so an apparently safe decision can still be followed by an unexpected encounter. [Moving-unit rules](UNIT_ENCOUNTERS.md).

Choosing a command does not spend resources, consume random values, reveal land or mutate the game. The desktop executes and records the same ordinary commands as manual play. The policy is a survival-oriented heuristic with normal hunting and encounter risks; it is not an optimal strategy or a survival guarantee. Companions support and follow their owners rather than receiving separate autoplay orders.

## Stopping, saving and older stories

Autoplay stops when the controlled tribe is extinct, a decision fails to advance the game, or the 20,000-command story capacity is reached. Older single-band rules retain their original terminal condition. The same command limit applies to manual play so the record remains saveable. No autosaves are created. The final account can be saved, read or dismissed to survey the map.

Current stories use V9 to preserve initial personality, terrain, tribe, salt, naming and encounter rules. Band orders record their actor and any animal or band target IDs; saves contain no special autoplay instructions. Untouched daughters can make a deterministic voluntary journey at turn close; any successful order, including Wait/Hold, prevents that for the current turn. Older files replay their original rules and commands exactly. The normal Load flow then records current rule introductions for a surviving story when command capacity allows, including encounters needed for tribal play and the new dispositions. Existing independent peoples remain independent; future splits create controlled tribal households.

Core checks cover legal commands, partial turns, food and salt survival, animal care, shelter, encounters, fission, reunion, succession, weighted travel, hidden-information independence, state/RNG purity and exact replay. Native checks cover band selection, follow-camera behavior, manual takeover, event sheets, useful-place controls, salt ledgers and terminal accounts.
