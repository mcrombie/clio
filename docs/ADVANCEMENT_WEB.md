# Clio — advancement as lived practice

**Status:** proposed pre-bronze content specification, v0.1. These 28 nodes are a design target. The executable prototype implements a smaller set of simplified milestones, not this entire carrier, maintenance, and transmission model. All quantities below are tunable game rules, not historical claims or animal husbandry advice.

## 1. Rules shared by every node

An advancement is a community's ability to reproduce a useful practice under known conditions. It is not an abstract purchase. A civilization can remember a practice it can no longer perform, acquire it through willing teachers, or lose its last active carriers while descendants elsewhere preserve it.

The starting fifty people already speak, make basic tools, use fire, forage, and care for each other. Early nodes represent durable improvements to organization and reliability; they do not imply that fire or language is first invented on turn one.

### Prerequisite notation

- `ALL {A, B}` means both A and B must be actively available to the practicing community, locally or through a reliable participating teacher.
- `ANY {C, D}` means at least one of C or D must also be available.
- `NONE` imposes no requirement in that category. An empty `ANY` must never be interpreted as impossible.
- Prerequisites are **capabilities**, not political ownership of a node elsewhere. A remote annexed village cannot automatically satisfy them.
- Every listed evidence condition is conjunctive unless the text explicitly says “either.” Thresholds count distinct credited episodes, not button presses.

The web has intentionally intersecting paths. It has no universal “finish hunting, then unlock farming” sequence. IDs are stable content identifiers; their numeric order is not an age rank or a strict topological order.

### State, evidence, and commitment

Every local practice moves through **unfamiliar → observed → practiced → reproducible → transmitted**. Reproducible means the community can reliably use the capability. Transmitted means it has at least two independent carrier groups and has successfully taught another group. **Dormant** preserves memory and provenance without current output benefits; **locally lost** means the remaining records cannot support recovery without new practical learning or a teacher.

Record evidence as events with stable IDs, actors, turn, place, context, consumed inputs, outcome, and counterparty or wildlife lineage. An event may legitimately inform several practices, but each requirement credits that event only once. “Distinct sites” must differ in the relevant ecology or task, not merely be two coordinates in the same abundant patch.

The commitment is concrete: time, materials, food, transport, skilled care, an access agreement, or an organizational burden. Evidence generated by ordinary work does not cost an additional abstract currency. Where a special project is necessary, preview its inputs and what the reserved labor will stop doing. In the two-AP prototype, a work action is one band action; in the fuller model, work becomes a share of available household labor. Never grant full-population work for every tiny split band.

Successful teaching can replace rediscovery. A learner needs accessible teachers, any material or environmental prerequisites, and two successful local trials before using an imported practice reliably. Breeding history belongs to an animal lineage and travels with documented stock; animal care still needs local validation. Teaching cannot manufacture animals, seeds, buildings, or an access treaty.

Bounded experiments are available before a practice is reproducible whenever its prerequisites and physical inputs permit them. A seed trial must be possible before garden expertise; a one-off agreement must be possible before a formal assembly; managed animals must be able to reproduce before a domestic lineage is recognized. Nodes improve reliability, available planning tools, and organizational scale. They must not create a circular rule in which players need the completed advancement to perform its qualifying evidence.

### Anti-farming rules

1. Credit at most one qualifying practice episode of the same kind per community per turn. Splitting or merging communities preserves evidence ancestry and does not refresh a turn's credit.
2. Movement must involve a meaningful journey, seasonal return, hazard, or exchange. Walking back and forth across an edge is not exploration evidence.
3. Real costs and ecological stocks apply even while pursuing milestones. Food given to wolves is removed; hunted animals die; harvested stands need renewal.
4. A staged failure followed by an immediate repair cannot repeatedly earn a new adverse-context achievement. Credit comparable recovery evidence once per actual environmental episode.
5. Duplicate treaties, gifts returned immediately, and circular trade do not create new counterparties or sustained obligations. Count independent participating communities and fulfilled benefits.
6. Do not require undisclosed random luck to fill an opaque checklist. Show controllable missing conditions; show residual chance and evidence preserved after failure.
7. Reproduction evidence must reference actual offspring events with viable parents, inputs, survival, and the intended relationship to people. Summoning, purchasing, or repeatedly renaming the same animal is not local breeding evidence.
8. Inactivity alone does not erase a healthy oral tradition every few turns. Dormancy follows missing carriers, unavailable materials, failed maintenance, or broken practical continuity. Ordinary use and teaching maintain skills automatically.

### Local variants and maintenance

A practice instance records its founding and teaching communities, relevant ecology, variant traits, carriers, confidence, and last practical validation. A variant changes a bounded concrete behavior—for example, dry-climate curing is efficient locally but requires testing in humid weather. It does not add a generic culture-strength score.

Default maintenance policy: loss of all capable carriers suspends active benefits immediately, while retained apprentices or oral memory enable recovery. A community may preserve an unused practice as oral memory for at least twelve turns before a separate loss check becomes eligible. Reliable teaching refreshes that window. Materials and animals can fail sooner; the UI distinguishes “the animals died” from “we forgot how to keep them.”

Exact windows belong in content data. Permanent upkeep chores for twenty-eight nodes would be poor play: normal work should satisfy most maintenance automatically, and the interface should surface only vulnerable or contested practices.

## 2. Web overview

| Route | Main nodes | What it can support without compulsory farming |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traveling knowledge | P02 → P08 → P10; P04 → P05 | Reliable seasonal migration and winter survival |
| Hunting and keeping food | P03 → P06 → P07 → P09 | Mobile or returning communities with buffered subsistence |
| Companion / herd relationships | P11 → P12; P13 → P15 → P14 | Dogs, pasture circuits, and managed herds |
| Water and abundant places | P16 → P17 → P22 | Fishing communities and villages without crop cultivation |
| Managed plants | P18 → P19 → P20; P21 | Gardens and food processing, alongside gathering |
| Common institutions | P26 plus recurring exchanges; P25, P27 | Confederacies, distributed teaching, and shared stores |
| Durable centers | P22 → P24 → P28 | Small towns with imported and local food support |

The reciprocal-exchange label above describes gameplay, not a circular prerequisite. All formal prerequisites are the explicit `ALL` and `ANY` declarations below. P14 can be reached through a pasture circuit or a secure stationary cache; P25 can arise through a village or an assembly. Distinct paths should produce different local histories even when they reach a similar capability.

## 3. The twenty-eight nodes

### P01 — Fire continuity

**ALL:** NONE. **ANY:** NONE.

**Evidence:** Carry or preserve a live fire through three distinct travel or weather episodes; one must involve rain, cold, or an exposed journey. Starting with a fire does not itself count as three successes.

**Commitment:** Reserve dry fuel, a protected ember container or equivalent local method, and the attention of one knowledgeable carrier during the difficult episode. Those supplies occupy transport capacity.

**Effect:** Access reliable fire at the next camp when local fuel and the chosen protection method suffice. Reduce exposure and fire-start delays; do not multiply food production. Opens more reliable preservation and shelter work.

**Maintain / variants:** Fuel depletion or loss of the fire still matters. A dry-grass carrying method needs local trials in a wet forest. Another household can learn by helping on real camp changes.

### P02 — Reading seasonal patches

**ALL:** NONE. **ANY:** NONE.

**Evidence:** Observe and use three ecologically distinct patches; return to one after its seasonal state changes; correctly anticipate at least one depletion or renewal event.

**Commitment:** Spend one observation action and preserve access to a return visit. The final prediction is made before the event; retrospective clicks do not count.

**Effect:** Show a two-step forecast range for familiar resource patches and distinguish depletion from temporary seasonal absence. Better information enables sustainable harvesting; it does not increase stocks by itself.

**Maintain / variants:** Knowledge is regional. The practice transfers as a way to observe, while forecasts in an unfamiliar climate remain uncertain until tested. Local teachers can supply regional experience.

### P03 — Repairable cutting toolkit

**ALL:** NONE. **ANY:** NONE.

**Evidence:** Obtain usable cutting material, produce and use tools in three work episodes, and successfully repair or replace a damaged tool using an identified supply source.

**Commitment:** One preparation action, a real material bundle, and a portion of carrying capacity. The repair consumes stock rather than silently regenerating equipment.

**Effect:** Lower waste from butchering and plant processing and permit more reliable material-working tasks. A proposed starting cap is a 10% reduction in relevant waste, not a blanket 10% output bonus.

**Maintain / variants:** Material access limits replacement. Local stone, bone, or other approved materials give differing durability and availability, with no single universally best source.

### P04 — Cordage and dependable bindings

**ALL:** NONE. **ANY:** NONE.

**Evidence:** Prepare usable fibers or sinew in two episodes; use bindings successfully in two different tasks, such as carrying and shelter fastening.

**Commitment:** A preparation action, harvested material, and drying or working conditions appropriate to the variant.

**Effect:** Enables tied frames, safer loads, nets or lines where locally learned, and more controllable animal handling. A small load-capacity improvement follows the actual equipment produced, not merely possessing the knowledge.

**Maintain / variants:** Wetness, wear, and material supply affect the equipment. New environments can support alternate fibers after a trial rather than restarting the entire practice.

### P05 — Portable weather shelters

**ALL:** P01, P04. **ANY:** NONE.

**Evidence:** Build a portable shelter, establish it at two genuinely different camps, and keep its occupants safe through one forecast difficult-weather episode.

**Commitment:** Two work actions across the project, hides or equivalent cover, frame materials, and a visible carried load. Moving still takes work; carrying a shelter is not free.

**Effect:** Reduce camp setup and exposure costs on an established migration. Makes leaving a durable center more practical without awarding a movement-speed increase everywhere.

**Maintain / variants:** Repair worn covers through ordinary camp work. Forest rain covers, steppe wind shelters, and upland insulation trade weight against protection.

### P06 — Coordinated drives and hunts

**ALL:** P02, P03. **ANY:** P04, P08.

**Evidence:** Observe a target herd's behavior in two episodes, complete two hunts involving distinct complementary roles, and successfully end or disengage from one hunt when the risk exceeds the declared plan.

**Commitment:** Commit enough effective labor for scouts and hunters; prepare equipment and a safe retreat. A tiny group cannot claim all roles without people to fill them.

**Effect:** Unlock planned drives with selectable risk, target size, and retreat conditions. Better coordination changes success and casualty distributions; the remaining herd stock still bounds yield.

**Maintain / variants:** Relevant animal experience matters. A forest ambush tradition does not fully transfer to enormous open-country prey. A successful cautious withdrawal is knowledge, not a failure to grind another kill.

### P07 — Drying, curing, and protected food

**ALL:** P01, P03. **ANY:** P02, P06.

**Evidence:** Preserve food from two separate harvests and consume one batch after a delay of two turns without spoilage or illness. Batch IDs prevent splitting one harvest into artificial achievements.

**Commitment:** Spend a processing action, fuel or appropriate drying conditions, and the original food. Set aside a proposed 20% processing loss for basic methods in content tuning; trade that loss against lower later spoilage.

**Effect:** Convert suitable fresh food into a portable preserved stock. Enables delayed consumption and longer journeys. It never creates provisions from nothing.

**Maintain / variants:** Humidity, temperature, pests, and storage conditions affect the result. Imported methods need local validation; drying, smoking, and other approved variants need not share exactly the same inputs.

### P08 — Remembered routes and return rights

**ALL:** P02. **ANY:** NONE.

**Evidence:** Complete a route linking at least three meaningful locations; return after a seasonal change; verify water, a viable camp, and access along the path. Adjacent-edge oscillation does not qualify.

**Commitment:** An actual journey, a route-teaching action, and agreement or uncontested access where other communities use the corridor.

**Effect:** Show route food and exposure forecasts; reduce uncertainty and repeat scouting costs on that route. Designate seasonal destinations for semi-nomadic groups.

**Maintain / variants:** Floods, conflict, resource shifts, and lost access can invalidate particular segments without erasing navigation knowledge. Teachers carry route histories between related communities.

### P09 — Secure caches

**ALL:** P03, P04. **ANY:** P07, P08.

**Evidence:** Build a cache, place at least half a band-consumption turn of actual food in it, leave it unattended for two turns, and retrieve viable stock. The same food cannot remain simultaneously in the band inventory.

**Commitment:** Two work actions, a suitable place, containers or protective materials, and food unavailable during the test. Record who can retrieve or guard it.

**Effect:** Create a located inventory with lower spoilage and theft exposure than abandoned loose supplies. Multiple caches can support a route or a village.

**Maintain / variants:** Flooding, pests, theft, and damage remain possible and forecastable where known. Burial, raised platforms, and dry shelters have different site requirements; caches do not automatically assert sovereignty over their surroundings.

### P10 — Lean-phase provisioning

**ALL:** P02. **ANY:** P05, P07, P09.

**Evidence:** Publish a plan before a forecast lean phase, maintain sufficient expected reserves or access along the plan, and complete three difficult-phase episodes without a preventable food deficit. Each episode must draw on real conditions rather than a user toggling weather.

**Commitment:** Reserve enough stock, movement capacity, or negotiated access to cover the forecast. Accept reduced current growth or discretionary work where necessary.

**Effect:** Enable household reserve targets, a ration preview, and planned seasonal departure. Resilience can come from migration, preservation, storage, or their combination.

**Maintain / variants:** Reforecast when population, access, or climate changes. This is a planning practice; it does not immunize the polity against a more severe event than its reserves can handle.

### P11 — Careful animal contact

**ALL:** P02. **ANY:** P06, P07, P09.

**Evidence:** Observe one identifiable, suitable animal group across at least four turns; complete three nonhostile encounters; recognize and respect at least one avoidance, stress, or feeding constraint before another attempt.

**Commitment:** Actual food or protection inputs, trained attention, and a chance of injury or lost provisions. Every encounter states the input at risk.

**Effect:** Unlock species-appropriate trust-building and handling actions with improved risk estimates. Animals remain wild or habituated; no domestic resource appears.

**Maintain / variants:** Trust belongs to the relevant animal group and people. A related community can learn the method but must establish its own relationship. Dragons and other unsuitable candidates do not become domestic merely by meeting a generic counter.

### P12 — Companion dog lineage

**ALL:** P11, P04. **ANY:** P05, P09.

**Evidence:** Accumulate ten materially costly positive contacts with a suitable wolf-derived founder group across at least twelve observation turns; record two viable managed reproduction episodes; successfully care for the group through one difficult local phase. Count credited successes at most once per community per turn.

**Commitment:** Retain healthy founders, reserve an explicit share of food, provide safe rearing space, and maintain two knowledgeable handlers or one handler plus an active apprentice. Offspring consume supplies before providing mature capabilities.

**Effect:** Create a lineage from the **actual surviving animals and offspring**, with founder provenance and one tested local specialization such as tracking, alerting, or camp defense. Trusted individual companions may have given smaller benefits before this node became reproducible.

**Maintain / variants:** Feed, health, human relationships, and breeding viability remain necessary. Traits change slowly and have tradeoffs: vigilance can mean noise and stress; cold tolerance can increase heat burden. Trading a dog transfers that animal, not a duplicate of the whole lineage.

### P13 — Herd handling

**ALL:** P11, P04. **ANY:** P08, P09.

**Evidence:** Identify a suitable herd species; guide or contain a healthy founder group on three distinct occasions; give it access to food and water; recover from one actual separation or escape without replacing the animals through a spawn reward.

**Commitment:** Handlers, barriers or a viable guiding route, and grazing access. Remove founders from the wild stock when they enter managed custody; record deaths and escapes.

**Effect:** Permit limited direction, protection, and selective retention of a managed group. This is handling, not proof of stable domestication or guaranteed breeding.

**Maintain / variants:** Captivity stress, predators, forage, and handler familiarity govern continuing control. Forest-edge pens and open-pasture guidance are different variants.

### P14 — Managed breeding herds

**ALL:** P13, P10. **ANY:** P15, P09.

**Evidence:** Ten positive care/handling contacts across at least twelve observation turns; a viable founder group; two successful managed reproduction episodes; no unresolved projected feed or water deficit through the next difficult local phase. Species suitability is a separate hard gate.

**Commitment:** Keep breeding animals rather than immediately consuming them, maintain handlers, and reserve pasture or stored feed. A proposed initial founder minimum is six suitable animals with viable reproduction roles; species data may require a different count.

**Effect:** Establish a tracked domestic herd lineage. Produce offspring only through the animal lifecycle. Herd value comes from actual animals, planned offtake, permitted products, and exchange; the node never grants “100 cattle.”

**Maintain / variants:** Overharvesting, narrow founder diversity, infection, or poor feed can reduce viability. Trait selection trades size, endurance, temperament, and environmental tolerance. Imported animals can enlarge a herd and may create a documented mixed lineage rather than silently replace its origin.

### P15 — Pasture circuits

**ALL:** P08, P13, P10. **ANY:** NONE.

**Evidence:** Guide a managed herd between three pasture/water locations through a seasonal transition; demonstrate that at least one used pasture renews; complete the circuit without a preventable feed deficit.

**Commitment:** Route access, mobile shelter or viable camp sites, handlers, and restraint in how much of each pasture is consumed. Negotiate rights when the circuit crosses other communities' use areas.

**Effect:** Schedule herd migration and forecast feed demand. Supports a mobile political economy without fields, pottery, or permanent houses.

**Maintain / variants:** Access disputes or climatic shifts can break a circuit. Local grazers, water spacing, and winter refuge create regional methods. The node can precede P14, representing skilled management before a domestic lineage is established.

### P16 — Skin or frame watercraft

**ALL:** P03, P04. **ANY:** NONE.

**Evidence:** Build an actual craft, complete three successful water journeys including one loaded crossing, and repair observed wear. Journeys must connect meaningful landings or work sites.

**Commitment:** Frame and covering materials, two work actions, competent operators, and carrying or mooring provision. Risk the cargo that is actually loaded.

**Effect:** Unlock suitable sheltered-water crossings and water-based transport with explicit capacity and weather limits. Ordinary early craft do not enable safe open-ocean or polar travel.

**Maintain / variants:** River, marsh, and sheltered-coast craft can differ in draft, portability, capacity, and durability. Operators need local trials before using an unfamiliar kind in different water.

### P17 — Returning fish runs and fixed traps

**ALL:** P02, P04. **ANY:** P09, P16.

**Evidence:** Correctly anticipate a run or aquatic renewal event, install a viable trap or equivalent access method, harvest across three episodes, and leave sufficient breeders or opening intervals for an observed renewal.

**Commitment:** Site-specific materials, two construction/repair actions, access to the water, and foregone maximum harvest during the renewal test.

**Effect:** Establish predictable, labor-efficient gathering opportunities at particular aquatic sites. This can support substantial returning communities without crop cultivation.

**Maintain / variants:** Floods, blocked channels, overharvest, and upstream activity affect output. The method is local; a river weir does not automatically work in a lake or sea.

### P18 — Tended useful stands

**ALL:** P02. **ANY:** P03, P09.

**Evidence:** Identify a useful plant patch, perform three appropriate tending episodes such as selective clearance or protection, and observe a later yield or regeneration benefit compared with the recorded untended expectation.

**Commitment:** Labor now for a delayed benefit, constrained harvesting, and continued access. Fire-based tending is only available where the local method and risk model support it.

**Effect:** Improve a particular gathering landscape's reliability or composition. A community can tend a place it visits seasonally; residence is not required.

**Maintain / variants:** The effect weakens if the landscape changes or tending stops beyond its ecological interval. Useful trees, roots, and seed plants produce different obligations and benefits.

### P19 — Saved seed tradition

**ALL:** P18, P09. **ANY:** P07, P23.

**Evidence:** Select and store a real seed batch, preserve viable seed through an adverse or dormant phase, and obtain successful regrowth from that batch in two separate planting episodes.

**Commitment:** Keep food-grade seed out of consumption, protect it from moisture and pests, and dedicate care to regrowth trials. Batches track quantity and viability.

**Effect:** Carry a reproducible seed stock and establish locally adapted planting material. Seeds can be exchanged along with practical instruction; possession alone does not prove local suitability.

**Maintain / variants:** Failure can destroy the stock without erasing the selection method. Repeated harvesting and reseeding produce bounded trait changes, with yield, tolerance, and storage tradeoffs.

### P20 — Household garden plots

**ALL:** P18, P19, P10. **ANY:** NONE.

**Evidence:** Plant, tend, and harvest a plot over two successful growth episodes; show a positive net provision return after seed retention and necessary labor; complete one lean phase without consuming the whole seed reserve.

**Commitment:** Land access, viable seed, work during time-sensitive windows, and enough other food to wait for the crop. A plot is a located asset that cannot move with the band.

**Effect:** Add a cultivated food source with forecast yield and specific seasonal work obligations. Supports growth and storage, while creating opportunity costs and dependence on a place.

**Maintain / variants:** Missed tending, water stress, damaged soil, and seed loss matter. Rotation, fallow intervals, and complementary gathering can be introduced as variant choices; no infinite passive yield after one click.

### P21 — Deliberate plant processing

**ALL:** P03. **ANY:** P18, P19, P20.

**Evidence:** Process three real batches from at least two locally approved plant sources; demonstrate safe consumption and a measurable improvement in usable food or storage quality.

**Commitment:** A processing surface or tool, labor, and any recipe-specific water or heat. Heavy equipment imposes transport costs unless made at return sites.

**Effect:** Convert previously inefficient local harvests into usable provisions, with an explicit input-output recipe. Gathering communities can use the practice as readily as gardeners.

**Maintain / variants:** Methods are ingredient-specific. Do not infer that every unknown plant becomes edible or safe. Variant recipes add content without becoming universal research bonuses.

### P22 — Returning villages

**ALL:** P09, P10. **ANY:** P17, P18, P20.

**Evidence:** Use the same shared site in three occupation episodes, including a return after some residents depart; sustain at least two households with different work roles; restore stores or structures after an actual interval of use.

**Commitment:** Common access rules, repair contributions, protected stores, and a plan for residents or assets left behind. Communities may stay seasonally rather than year-round.

**Effect:** Establish a recognized settlement with shared facilities and a calendar of use. Group residents and visiting bands in one readable location panel. Settlement status does not increase every yield automatically.

**Maintain / variants:** Seasonal occupancy, full residence, and a maintained empty interval are valid. The settlement can be abandoned without deleting the inhabitants' history or forbidding future mobility.

### P23 — Fired earthen containers

**ALL:** P01, P02. **ANY:** P09, P08.

**Evidence:** Locate suitable material and fuel; make three batches with recorded firing outcomes; demonstrate that a successful batch stores or processes its intended contents through a two-turn trial.

**Commitment:** Clay-like material, fuel, preparation space, and two work actions. Failed vessels consume inputs; useful vessels have weight and breakage risk.

**Effect:** Produce containers with specific storage or cooking uses. Durable caches and settled stores benefit, while heavy vessels may be unattractive on a frequent migration.

**Maintain / variants:** Fuel cost, porousness, weight, and durability vary locally. A traveling community can maintain a pottery-making return site instead of carrying an entire workshop.

### P24 — Substantial houses and repair groups

**ALL:** P03, P04, P22. **ANY:** NONE.

**Evidence:** Build weather-appropriate houses for two resident household groups; occupy them through one difficult phase; complete a coordinated repair after ordinary wear or a real event.

**Commitment:** Several construction actions, substantial local material, site drainage or equivalent preparation, and ongoing repair labor. Record who can live in and inherit use of the structures.

**Effect:** Reduce recurring exposure and temporary-camp work for residents; support care and specialized work spaces. The benefit belongs to occupied structures, not every band of the polity.

**Maintain / variants:** Building type follows local climate, materials, and practice. Houses concentrate assets and make departure costly, but can be transferred, left, or reused.

### P25 — Common-store obligations

**ALL:** P09. **ANY:** P22, P26.

**Evidence:** At least two independently represented communities or household groups contribute identifiable stocks; complete three allocation episodes under a stated rule; resolve one real shortage or disagreement with a recorded outcome.

**Commitment:** Public rules for access, a recognized keeper or rotating role, an audit visible to contributors, and agreed consequences for missed obligations. Contributions remain physical stock at a location.

**Effect:** Enable shared reserve targets, relief allocations, and predictable contributions. A mobile assembly can maintain a common cache just as a village can maintain a store.

**Maintain / variants:** Unexplained withdrawals, unfair access, and unreliable contributions erode participation. Redistribution, household entitlements, and emergency-use agreements are alternatives with different political tradeoffs—not one best government upgrade.

### P26 — Seasonal assemblies and confederacy practice

**ALL:** P08, P10. **ANY:** P15, P22, P27.

**Evidence:** At least two distinct communities meet in three episodes, exchange a material or protective benefit, and fulfill a reciprocal obligation across a separation. Merely splitting and immediately remerging the same band does not count.

**Commitment:** Travel or hosting costs, a known meeting place, delegates, interpreters where needed, and explicit limits on what the assembly may decide.

**Effect:** Offer confederacy agreements covering particular shared powers: routes, emergency aid, defense, stores, or dispute hearings. Members remain polities; direct control follows the agreed constitution rather than an automatic annexation.

**Maintain / variants:** Missed obligations prompt negotiation or withdrawal. Kin assembly, pasture league, and river council can all instantiate this practice. Broad geographic reach may compensate for low settlement density.

### P27 — Reciprocal teaching and guest households

**ALL:** P08. **ANY:** P10, P12, P17, P20.

**Evidence:** Host or send willing learners across a community boundary; teach a reproducible practice; observe two successful local trials by the learners after separation; maintain a reciprocal visit or recognized support obligation.

**Commitment:** Food, accommodation, travel, and teacher time; language mediation if needed. Learners retain their political and cultural identities unless other events change them.

**Effect:** Establish durable teaching routes and extra independent carriers. Supports knowledge survival and bilingual contact without requiring conquest, biological intermarriage, or permanent assimilation.

**Maintain / variants:** Personal ties and guest safety matter. A break in relations can suspend the route while taught knowledge remains. Basic teaching is available before this node; the advancement formalizes intercommunity continuity.

### P28 — Small-town stewardship

**ALL:** P22, P24, P25. **ANY:** P14, P17, P20.

**Evidence:** Sustain a proposed 160 residents across at least three neighborhood groups for six turns; maintain at least two distinct food supply chains; support two necessary non-food roles through contributions; resolve a concrete shared-space or sanitation problem without a chronic reserve deficit.

**Commitment:** Regular communal work, managed waste and water access, accountable allocation, and arrangements with suppliers. Residents performing public duties still consume provisions.

**Effect:** Unlock neighborhood-level administration, shared service projects, and delegated offices. This makes dense residence manageable; it does not create food or make mobile neighbors culturally inferior.

**Maintain / variants:** Dependence on imports, disease exposure, political exclusion, and failed maintenance remain risks. The population threshold is a game-management trigger, not a historical definition of a town. A shrinking community can retain buildings and memories while retiring unnecessary offices.

## 4. Age summaries without a universal ladder

Use a four-part **development profile** instead of a single technology score. Each dimension ranges from 0–4, derived from actual active arrangements. The values are descriptive and give no production, combat, or diplomatic bonuses.

| Dimension | 0–1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subsistence resilience | One dominant immediate source; limited reserve | A maintained buffer or an independent fallback | Two maintained sources with different failure modes | Distributed fallback arrangements demonstrated through disruption |
| Coordination reach | One camp or closely co-resident households | Two separated communities fulfilling an agreement | Several member communities with a functioning assembly | Multiple overlapping agreements with delegated roles and resolved disputes |
| Investment permanence | Portable equipment or occasional return sites | Maintained caches and recurring shared places | Durable occupied settlements and infrastructure | Interdependent neighborhoods and shared services |
| Transmission resilience | One group of carriers | Multiple active carriers locally | Successful intercommunity teaching | Several independent carrier communities with demonstrated recovery after loss |

Chronicle chapter suggestions:

- **First Bearings:** P02 plus one of P01/P03/P04 becomes reproducible. The community can name how it survives locally.
- **Remembered Returns:** resilience reaches 2, and either P08 or P22 is active. A moving circuit and a returning settlement can both qualify.
- **Interwoven Communities:** coordination reach reaches 3 and transmission resilience reaches 3. P26 and P27 are common routes, without houses or gardens as universal requirements.
- **Durable Centers:** permanence reaches 4 with P28 active. This is a settlement chapter a campaign may never pursue.

Do not display “Durable Centers” as the winner above “Interwoven Communities.” A polity may have both, and different member communities can occupy different descriptions. The world can enter a chapter when such arrangements appear somewhere, while the player's local profile continues to describe its own history. A chapter persists in the chronicle after its arrangements collapse; current capability is shown separately.

## 5. A deliberately mobile path and a settled-gathering path

**Mobile league:** P02 → P08, with P01 + P04 → P05 and P10. P06/P07 support provision buffers. Animal contact opens P13, then P15; P14 becomes possible after reproduction and winter care. P27 and P26 formalize teaching and assemblies. P25 can establish a seasonal common store through P26. This route requires no P19, P20, P23, P24, or P28. Its costs are transport, corridors, herd care, hosting, and maintaining reciprocal access.

**Settled gathering community:** P02/P03/P04 establish the basics; P07/P09 buffer food, while P16/P17 organize water access and fish runs. P10 and P22 support a returning village. P24/P25 permit durable residence and accountable stores. P28 can use P17 as its food capability prerequisite, so a town does not require cultivated crops. Its costs are dependence on particular runs, repair, crowding, and relationships with suppliers.

**Mixed path:** One community gardens at a river store while another follows a pasture circuit. They can share P25 and P26 without sharing every practice or language form. A drought may make seasonal departure attractive; preserved routes and portable shelter make that decision practical. This is where Clio's political and knowledge systems should connect most strongly.

## 6. Content and implementation contract

Each node should be content data with a stable ID, localized title and explanation, prerequisite sets, environmental predicates, evidence requirements, commitment recipes, unlocked actions, effect scopes, maintenance conditions, and variant hooks. Keep UI prose out of the deterministic evaluator. Store state and evidence per community/practice instance; derive polity summaries rather than writing capability directly onto the polity.

Evaluation order is:

1. Determine whether the practice's prerequisites and environmental constraints are currently satisfied.
2. Filter evidence by stable IDs, provenance, dates, distinct relevant contexts, and anti-farming rules.
3. Confirm concrete project inputs and obligations; reserve them atomically when the player commits.
4. Apply successful outputs at their actual locations and to their actual owners.
5. Record carriers, local confidence, variant provenance, and teaching state.
6. Recheck maintenance when relevant events occur; publish only meaningful status changes.

A reproducible practice should expose three concise explanations: **why it is available**, **what is still needed**, and **what would put it at risk**. The player can inspect deeper evidence, but routine play should not require reading a ledger of every forage action.

### Required validation before promoting a node from design to implemented

- Valid prerequisites with explicit all/any behavior; no accidental requirement for every branch in an `ANY` set.
