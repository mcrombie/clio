# Historical founding inspirations — experimental

Clio can begin with an imagined culture or one of three historical language inspirations. A preset supplies twelve game roots, a distinct letter inventory, and a fictional default band name. The player may replace that name. Later sound changes, place names, political developments, and histories emerge through the existing fictional simulation.

The default new-game choice is now **Zhol**, Michael's custom Chinese–Spanish sound fusion. It is documented separately in [ZHOL.md](ZHOL.md); the three historical options below retain their released vocabulary.

These are **playable inspirations, not reconstructed proto-languages or reconstructions of prehistoric societies**. The source vocabulary comes from much later surviving language evidence. Choosing a template does not claim that a fifty-person wandering band spoke an attested historical language, or that every community in a region shared one culture.

| Choice | Language label | Fictional default band |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An imagined culture | Generated language | Generated from the seed and sound style |
| Early Chinese inspiration | Old Chinese-inspired | Krong-ming |
| Early Egyptian inspiration | Early Egyptian-inspired | Iteru-remet |
| Sumerian inspiration | Sumerian-inspired | Id-lu |

The Sumerian choice represents one language inspiration from Mesopotamia. It is not a universal “Proto-Mesopotamian” language. Penn's dictionary project treats Sumerian as a distinct language rather than a generic label for all Mesopotamian speech. [Penn Museum: Electronic Pennsylvania Sumerian Dictionary](https://www.penn.museum/research/project.php?pid=17)

## What the presets affect

- The player's first founding language, its inherited naming roots, and its provenance label.
- The initial default band name, when no custom name is supplied.
- The fictional vocabulary inherited by later daughter languages.

They do not change terrain, climate, food yields, wildlife, starting location, population, knowledge, ancestry, survival odds, or the band's behavioral culture values. In a four-founder game, the other three founding languages and bands retain their ordinary generated settings. This avoids prescribing abilities or personalities to real peoples.

Historical founding roots are fixed so they remain recognizable across seeds. The seed still determines the world, other generated founders, and subsequent simulation. Choosing a different sound style affects generated languages; it does not reroll the selected historical lexicon.

## Old Chinese inspiration

The starting references are Baxter and Sagart's own supplementary reconstruction data. Clio strips scholarly notation, compresses some consonants into ordinary Latin letters, omits uncertain prefixes or suffixes, and selects one approximate game root per concept. The results are not Mandarin, phonetic transcriptions, or a competing scholarly reconstruction. [Baxter–Sagart project and downloads](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/)

| Game concept | Game root | Lexical inspiration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| water | stur | 水 |
| river | krong | 江; the river name is generalized |
| mountain | sngrar | 山 |
| forest | mok | 木; tree/wood is generalized |
| people | ming | 民 |
| home | kra | 家; household is generalized |
| wolf | rang | 狼 |
| cattle | ngwe | 牛 |
| fire | khwej | 火 |
| sun | nik | 日 |
| stone | dak | 石 |
| grass | tsen | 荐; a plant/fodder sense is generalized |

The source forms and their uncertainty conventions are in the authors' [2014 reconstruction list](https://ocbaxtersagart.lsait.lsa.umich.edu/BaxterSagartOCbyMandarinMC2014-09-20.pdf), with an alternative [radical ordering](https://ocbaxtersagart.lsait.lsa.umich.edu/BaxterSagartOCbyRadStr2014-09-20.pdf). Their [methodological discussion](https://tangcenter-columbia.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/BaxterSagartDraft2015-11-09.pdf) also explains distinctions that these game spellings cannot retain, including the special vowel notation associated with the stone root.

`ng`, `kh`, and consonant clusters are ordinary letter sequences in this prototype. They are not modeled as historical phonemes. The five-vowel game alphabet collapses scholarly vowel distinctions. `Krong-ming` and every generated compound are fictional constructions, not attested community names or validated Old Chinese grammar.

## Egyptian inspiration

The reference forms below come from the Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae, maintained by the Berlin-Brandenburg and Saxon academies. Each game spelling adds or changes sounds to fit Clio's Latin-letter engine. The selected lexical senses can span different periods; “early” describes the scenario's inspiration, not a claim that this table reconstructs Old Egyptian.

Egyptian scripts generally omit vowels, and the earlier language's sound remains partly uncertain. Clio's vowels are deliberate game inventions; familiar-looking spellings are not evidence of historical pronunciation. [UCL: Egyptian writing system](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/writing/system.html), [UCL: language and sound](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/museums-static/digitalegypt/sound/index.html)

| Game concept | Game root | Source and deliberate adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| water | mu | [mw](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/69000); invented vowel treatment |
| river | iteru | [jtr.w](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/33370); added vowels |
| mountain | dju | [ḏw](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/182830); simplified consonants and vowels |
| forest | khetu | [ḫt](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/121200); wood/tree generalized, ending invented |
| people | remet | [rmṯ.t](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/94550); compressed consonants and added vowels |
| home | per | [pr](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/60220); house generalized, vowel added |
| wolf | wenesh | [wnš](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/867401); **jackal** used as a fictional canid proxy |
| cattle | ka | [kꜣ](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/162930); bull generalized, simplified spelling |
| fire | rekah | [rkḥ](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/96440); burning verb repurposed as a game noun |
| sun | ra | [rꜥw](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/93290); simplified spelling |
| stone | iner | [jnr](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/27560); added vowels |
| grass | semu | [sm.w](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/lemma/134140); plant/pasture sense generalized, vowels added |

In particular, the game does not claim that the jackal lemma is a precise translation of “wolf”, or that the burning verb is an Egyptian noun for fire. These substitutions fill the same twelve gameplay categories used by generated languages. `Iteru-remet` is an invented compound, not an attested Egyptian population name.

## Sumerian inspiration

The short roots draw on Sumerian lexicography. Accents and sign-reading indices are omitted; grammatical endings and semantic distinctions are simplified. The source dictionaries remain authoritative for their own entries; this twelve-root list is only a game adaptation. [Penn Museum: dictionary project](https://www.penn.museum/research/project.php?pid=17)

| Game concept | Game root | Adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| water | a | Short lexical root |
| river | id | Shortened reading |
| mountain | kur | One sense selected |
| forest | tir | One sense selected |
| people | lu | Person generalized to community |
| home | e | House generalized; reading mark omitted |
| wolf | urbar | Shortened form |
| cattle | gud | Bull/ox generalized |

The individual lexical inspirations can be checked in John A. Halloran's [Sumerian Lexicon, version 3](https://www.sumerian.org/sumerian.pdf), especially the short-root entries. Corpus examples independently support [forest](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/dcclt/signlists/cbd/sux/T.html), [fire](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/epsd2/sux/o0031365), and [wolf](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/cams/akno/cbd/sux/x000004300.html). The game compound `Id-lu` and later place names are fictional and do not implement Sumerian morphology.

## Engine limits and provenance

`LanguageProfile.Template` records the founding inspiration. Branching and borrowing preserve the recipient's template, while parent/root IDs and loan etymons continue to track ancestry and contact separately. This is provenance, not a claim that a later fictional branch is a real historical descendant.

The palettes in `HistoricalCultures.cs` are letter inventories used by the existing substitution and loan-adaptation engine. They are not complete historical phonological inventories. The generated language builder still uses its original CV syllables; fixed historical-inspired roots can include clusters, vowel-initial words, and different shapes. Later changes apply the existing fictional letter substitutions consistently, not historical sound laws tailored to these three languages.

All eight legacy fingerprints in `HistoricalCultureChecks` were captured from the original `atlas-02` simulation DLL: four initial worlds and their twelve-turn replays. They include public state, private random generators, vocabulary, names, and progress fields, while ignoring only newly added descriptive provenance. The original five-argument `Game` constructor and explicit `Generated` setting preserve those fingerprints.

Historical stories use `CLIO-STORY-2`, which stores the chosen template before replaying commands. **The released roots, inventories, default names, template identifiers, and their interpretation are frozen replay data for this save version.** A future correction must retain a compatible version of these presets or migrate/version the save format; silently editing a table would change existing stories on load. Improvements to scholarly wording in this document can proceed independently of the replay data. Generated stories retain the original `CLIO-STORY-1` behavior.

Sources checked 2026-09-04. A future expansion should separately model historical phonemes, dated lexicons, morphology, writing systems, and region-specific evidence before claiming greater historical precision.
